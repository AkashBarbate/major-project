ABSTRACT
--------

The volume of news content published online every day has grown to a point where manual organization and verification are no longer practical. Readers frequently encounter articles that span multiple topics, yet most classification systems force each article into a single category. At the same time, unreliable or misleading content continues to spread across digital platforms, and very few tools exist that help ordinary readers gauge whether a piece of news can be trusted.

This project presents NewsLens, a system that tackles both of these problems together. Given any English news article, it identifies all relevant categories from a set of eight predefined labels and simultaneously assigns a credibility score between 0 and 100 that reflects how trustworthy the content appears to be.

The backbone of the classification module is a RoBERTa-base transformer that was fine-tuned on a custom dataset of 3,019 articles scraped from over 460 distinct domains. Because off-the-shelf news datasets tend to be outdated and heavily skewed toward a few topics, every article in our corpus was collected through RSS feeds, Common Crawl archives, and targeted web scraping, then annotated with multi-label categories using a Gemini-based LLM pipeline. A five-stage cleaning process, including domain capping, deduplication, and short-article removal, reduced the raw pool of 5,248 scraped articles down to 2,947 balanced samples ready for training.

Rather than applying a single fixed decision threshold of 0.5, we used Optuna to tune an independent threshold for each of the eight categories, which pushed the macro F1-score from 0.8428 on the baseline DistilBERT model to approximately 0.91 on the final RoBERTa model. Temperature calibration further improved probability reliability, cutting the Expected Calibration Error by 62 percent.

For credibility, we designed a lightweight module that extracts eight linguistic signals, including subjectivity, clickbait patterns, sensational vocabulary, and source reputation, and combines them through a weighted formula to produce the final score.

The complete system is deployed as a FastAPI backend with a Streamlit frontend, allowing users to paste text or provide a URL and receive classification results, credibility assessments, and keyword-level explanations powered by Integrated Gradients in real time.

Keywords: multi-label classification, news credibility, RoBERTa, transformer, Optuna, threshold optimization, explainable AI, NLP
