CHAPTER 3: PROBLEM DEFINITION AND OBJECTIVES
===============================================

3.1 Problem Definition
-----------------------

The core problem this project addresses is the gap between how news articles are written and how existing systems organize them. In the real world, news content is inherently multi-topic. A single article about a government initiative to fund cancer research in public universities simultaneously involves Politics, Health, and Education. Despite this, virtually every deployed news classification system forces each article into one and only one category, discarding the secondary topics entirely.

This single-label constraint has two direct consequences for end users. First, articles are hidden from readers who would find them relevant but happen to be following a different category. Second, recommendation systems that rely on these single-label predictions propagate the error downstream, narrowing the range of content that users see over time.

Alongside the classification problem, there is the question of credibility. The barrier to publishing content online is essentially zero. Any individual or organization can produce articles that look professionally formatted but contain misleading claims, sensationalized language, or unverified information. Readers have no systematic tool to quickly assess how much trust to place in a given piece of content. Most platforms display articles without any indication of source reliability or writing quality.

To address both of these challenges, the project defines two formal tasks. The first task is Multi-label Classification, where given a news article A, the system predicts a subset L of labels from the set containing Politics, Science and Technology, Business and Economy, International Affairs, Education, Health, Sports, and Entertainment, such that L contains all and only the categories that are genuinely relevant to A. The second task is Credibility Scoring, where for the same article A, the system computes a credibility score C in the range of 0 to 100 based on measurable linguistic features and source characteristics. A higher score indicates that the article exhibits patterns commonly associated with reliable journalism, while a lower score flags potential quality concerns.


3.2 Objectives
---------------

To solve the problems defined above, the project pursues seven specific objectives.

The first objective focuses on custom dataset construction. Instead of relying on publicly available datasets, the project builds a diverse, balanced training corpus by scraping news articles from multiple Indian and international sources. Domain capping, deduplication, and quality filtering are applied to ensure that no single source or category dominates the dataset.

The second objective addresses scalable multi-label annotation. An LLM-assisted annotation pipeline using Gemini and Gemma models is developed to automatically assign multi-label categories to each article. Fallback mechanisms are included for ambiguous or low-confidence cases.

The third objective involves transformer model selection and training. Candidate transformer architectures are evaluated, and the selected model, RoBERTa-base, is fine-tuned for multi-label classification using sigmoid activation and Binary Cross-Entropy loss. Training strategies are employed to handle noisy labels and class imbalance.

The fourth objective is per-category threshold optimization. Optuna-based Bayesian optimization is used to discover the optimal classification threshold for each of the eight categories independently, moving beyond the standard uniform 0.5 cutoff.

The fifth objective centers on credibility scoring. A credibility module is designed and implemented that computes a trustworthiness score using eight hand-crafted linguistic features, providing a transparent and explainable assessment.

The sixth objective is Explainable AI integration. Integrated Gradients-based attribution is incorporated to show users which specific words in an article contributed most to each predicted category, making the classification decisions interpretable.

The seventh objective covers end-to-end deployment. The complete system is built and deployed as a web application with a FastAPI REST API backend and an interactive Streamlit frontend, capable of processing raw text or URLs in real time.


3.3 Summary
-------------

This chapter formally defined the two tasks that NewsLens addresses: multi-label news classification and credibility scoring. The objectives outline the complete path from dataset creation through model training, optimization, and deployment that the following chapters describe in detail.
